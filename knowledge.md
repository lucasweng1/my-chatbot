## How do I say hello, goodbye, thank you and other basic greetings in French?
These are the basic greetings and polite phrases for a trip to France. Hello, good morning and good afternoon are all "Bonjour". Good evening is "Bonsoir". Excuse me is "Excusez-moi". Please is "S'il vous plaît". Thank you is "Merci". You're welcome is "De rien". Goodbye is "Au revoir". Yes is "Oui" and no is "Non". To introduce yourself, say "Je m'appelle…" followed by your name.

## How do I ask for help in French?
Here are French phrases for asking for help while traveling. "Do you speak English?" is "Parlez-vous anglais ?" "The bathroom, please?" is "Les toilettes, s'il vous plaît ?" "I'm sorry, I don't understand." is "Je suis désolé(e), je ne comprends pas." "Can you help me?" is "Pouvez-vous m'aider ?" "I'm lost." is "Je suis perdu(e)." Using these phrases is a simple way to get assistance from people you meet.

## How do I say where I want to go and ask where something is in French?
To get around town in France, you can use two basic patterns. "I want to go to ___" is "Je veux aller à ___." "Where is ___?" is "Où est ___ ?" Fill in the blank with the place you are looking for. The knowledge notes on French place names list useful words such as the museum, the beach, the train station and the city center, so you can complete these sentences.

## What are the French words for the museum, the beach, the train station and the city center?
Use these place names to fill in the blank in phrases like "Où est ___ ?" (Where is ___?) or "Je veux aller à ___." (I want to go to ___.) The museum is "le musée". The beach is "la plage". The train station is "la gare". The city center, also called downtown, is "le centre-ville". These four words cover common destinations when you are getting around town on a trip to France.

## How do I ask how much something costs or pay in French?
To ask the price, say "Ça coûte combien ?" which means "How much does it cost?" When you are ready to pay at a restaurant, say "L'addition, s'il vous plaît" for "The check, please." To say how you want to pay, use "en espèces" for cash or "par carte de crédit" for credit card. These phrases help you handle shopping and paying the bill during your trip to France.

## How do I order food and drinks in French?
Here are key phrases for ordering. A table for one, two, three or four people is "Une table pour une / deux / trois / quatre personne(s)." "I would like ___" is "Je voudrais ___." For more water, coffee, beer or wine, say "(Encore) de l'eau / du café / de la bière / du vin." "Cheers!" is "Santé !" "The menu, please" is "La carte, s'il vous plaît." "With" is "avec" and "without" is "sans".

## What are the French words for breakfast, lunch, dinner, appetizer, main course, dessert and pastry?
These French food words are useful when reading a menu or ordering. Breakfast is "le petit déjeuner". Lunch is "le déjeuner". Dinner is "le dîner". An appetizer can be "l'entrée", "l'amuse-bouche" or "le hors-d'œuvre". The main course is "le plat principal". Dessert is "le dessert". A pastry is "la viennoiserie". Knowing these helps you order confidently in places like a pâtisserie, which is a French pastry shop.

## When should I say bonjour or bonsoir, and how do I say have a good day in French?
It is considered polite to greet people with "bonjour" when you enter stores, cafés or souvenir shops during the day. In the evening, you start greeting people with "bonsoir" instead. When you are leaving a place, you can say "bonne journée" for "have a good day" or "bonne soirée" for "have a good evening." Using these greetings shows politeness to the people you meet.

## What does "truc" mean in French?
"Truc" is a casual French word for "thing", and the text says it is a favorite of the French, so you may overhear locals using it. An example is "C'est quoi ce truc ?" which means "What's this thing?" It is not a formal word, but it is common in everyday conversation. Knowing it can help you understand what locals say around you during your trip.

## What is apéro in France?
"Apéro" is short for "apéritif". It is a common tradition in France when people get together with friends or family. It means a pre-dinner drink, often served with small snacks. An example invitation is "Viens prendre l'apéro chez moi ce soir !" which means "Come have apéro at my place tonight!" If someone says this to you, you know you have made a French friend.

## Do I need to be fluent in French to travel in France?
No. You don't have to be fluent to have fun with French, and you are on vacation, so you shouldn't stress. Beginners can even have real conversations. Effort counts, because using some French shows locals that you are interested in their culture and community. The advice is to study smart, not hard: focus on the most useful words and phrases and worry less about getting every grammar detail right. A few key words go a long way.
